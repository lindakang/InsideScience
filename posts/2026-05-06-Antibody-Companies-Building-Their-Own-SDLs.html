Beyond the self-driving laboratories (SDLs) developed by academia and research institutions, the industry has inevitably joined the fray. After all, drug development is all about speed.

Currently, most SDLs are primarily applied to materials or small-molecule drugs. Although biologics have become increasingly prominent over the past decade or so, their manufacturing processes remain far more complex than those of small molecules. Nevertheless, several biotech companies have already started adopting SDLs. Additionally, major pharmaceutical companies are partnering with AI/ML firms to integrate SDL capabilities into their existing automated robotic systems. For instance, at the J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference (JPM) this January, Eli Lilly and NVIDIA announced a collaboration to build an AI factory.

Below is a brief introduction to companies developing antibody drugs using their own SDLs.

🌐 LabGenius Therapeutics

London-based LabGenius Therapeutics (founded in 2012) combines AI, robotics, and synthetic biology to engineer next-generation therapeutic antibodies, with a focus on multispecific formats. Its proprietary EVA™ platform is a fully closed-loop SDL.

EVA™ Platform

EVA™ is a highly automated, closed-loop SDL that integrates machine learning (ML), robotics, and synthetic biology to rapidly generate next-generation antibodies. Its Design–Build–Test–Learn cycle consists of two main components:

  • Dry Lab: ML models trained on experimental data to predict and design antibodies.
  • Automation Lab: Robotic systems that produce and test up to 2,300 multispecific antibodies per run.

Experimental results are fed back into the ML models for optimization, enabling continuous cycles of design, production, and testing. Each cycle takes less than 6 weeks, significantly accelerating the antibody development process compared to traditional approaches.

The platform supports a wide range of antibody modalities, including T-cell engagers (TCEs), antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs), NK-cell engagers, TCR-based engagers, and radioconjugates.

One of their lead candidates, LGTX-101, designed by EVA™, is a Nectin-4 × CD3 bispecific TCE currently advancing toward IND submission, with demonstrated >70,000-fold tumour selectivity.

Current updates

  • The company has raised multiple rounds of funding, including a £35 million Series B round in 2024.
  • In December 2025, LabGenius announced its second collaboration with Sanofi to optimize anti-inflammatory NANOBODY® therapeutics using the EVA™ platform.

🌐 BigHat Biosciences

Founded in 2019 in San Mateo, California, BigHat Biosciences develops antibodies across formats (IgG, multispecific IgG, scFv, ADCs, TCEs, VHH). Its Milliner™ platform integrates the RADS AI system, Reccy™ LIMS, and a fully automated wet lab to create an end-to-end SDL.

  • RADS (Reccy Antibody Design Studio): Models are initially trained on small datasets, then continuously updated using large volumes of high-quality experimental data generated weekly by the Milliner platform. The system automatically selects the best-performing models to design the next round of antibodies, using active learning for iterative optimization.
  • Automated lab: Handles antibody expression, purification, and functional testing, with results fed back into Reccy™.
  • Reccy™: A LIMS platform that controls instrumentation and integrates data from both RADS and experimental workflows.

This end-to-end loop, from training, learning, design, and testing to optimization (Design–Build–Test), can be completed in as little as one week.

Current updates

Most of their drugs in the pipeline target cancers, whereas BHB810 is an anti-CDH17 VHH-Fc ADC for gastric cancer. BigHat plans to start its Phase 1/2 this June. (Recent preclinical data was presented at the American Association for Cancer Research.)

In addition to internal discovery programs, BigHat collaborates with major pharmaceutical companies, including Johnson & Johnson, Merck, and Amgen.

🌐 AbSci

Publicly traded (NASDAQ: ABSI) AbSci, founded in 2011 in Vancouver, Washington, uses its Integrated Drug Creation™ Platform to discover and optimize de novo antibodies. The platform integrates generative AI and a wet lab to form a closed-loop SDL, with each design–test–optimize cycle taking approximately 6 weeks.

Integrated Drug Creation™ Platform

The key components of the platform include:

  • Origin-1: Generates novel antibody sequences, performs in silico screening, and analyzes results for optimization.
  • AbsciGen/AbsciBind: Antibody screening and prioritization.
  • Automated lab systems: Including antibody expression (SoluPro®), binding (SPR), and functional assays (ACE Assay™ and HiPrBind™ assays).

The full antibody development workflow is described in a recent publication (Levine et al., 2026):

  1. De novo generation: Using Origin-1 (formerly Denovium Engine™) and AbsciGen to generate novel antibody sequences.
  2. Evaluation and prediction: Using AbsciBind to score and prioritize candidates.
  3. Library construction: Designing and building customized antibody libraries.
  4. Expression: Producing antibodies using SoluPro®.
  5. Screening: Selecting candidates based on affinity, expression, performance, and quality using ACE Assay™ and HiPrBind™.
  6. Iterative optimization: Refining models with new data from each cycle.
  7. Scale-up and transfer: Optimizing manufacturing conditions and transferring processes to partners.

Pipeline

They have two candidates currently in the clinical stage:

  • ABS-101: An anti-TL1A antibody for inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). Although it has completed Phase 1, internal development has been paused in favour of seeking external partners.
  • ABS-201: An anti-prolactin receptor (PRLR) antibody for androgenetic alopecia. A Phase 1/2a trial (HEADLINE) began in December 2025, with early proof-of-concept data expected in the second half of 2026.

Partnerships & investors

AbSci collaborates with AstraZeneca and Merck (MSD). AMD has invested $20 million via PIPE financing in addition to AI-related collaborations. Other institutional investors include ARK, BlackRock, and Vanguard.

🌐 Insitro

Founded in 2018 in San Francisco by machine learning expert Daphne Koller, Insitro applies AI to biological discovery across metabolic disease, neuroscience, ophthalmology, and oncology through multiple platforms, including ChemML™, TherML™, POSH (Pooled Optical Screening in Human cells), Virtual Human™, and ClinML™.

By integrating automated laboratories, high-throughput experimental data, and clinical datasets, Insitro trains AI models to uncover disease mechanisms, identify therapeutic targets, and accelerate drug development across these areas.

In January this year, Insitro acquired the Israeli AI company CombinAbleAI, whose models specialize in antibody design and optimization. Integration into the TherML™ platform expands Insitro’s capabilities into a modality-agnostic AI system covering small molecules, oligonucleotides, and biologics.

  • ChemML™: Small-molecule drug discovery; integrated with automation to form an end-to-end closed-loop SDL.
  • CellML™: Generates and analyzes large-scale in vitro data.
  • ClinML™: Analyzes clinical datasets.
  • Virtual Human™: Integrates CellML™ and ClinML™ data to model disease mechanisms.
  • TherML™: Utilizing Virtual Human™ data and integrating the ChemML™ platform, TherML™ is a modality-agnostic system that autonomously learns, designs, analyzes, and optimizes small molecules, oligonucleotides, antibodies, and complex biologics. Combined with Insitro’s automated laboratories, it operates as an end-to-end closed-loop SDL.

Pipeline

Two siRNA candidates for MASH (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis), CTRO-1013 and CTRO-1029, are being developed in collaboration with Eli Lilly and are preparing for IND submission.

Partnerships & funding

In addition to Eli Lilly, Insitro collaborates with Bristol Myers Squibb on small-molecule therapies for ALS. From its Series A (2019) to Series C (2021), the company raised approximately $643 million. The Series C round was led by CPP (Canada Pension Plan) Investments, with participation from ARCH Venture Partners and SoftBank Investment Advisers.

Biologics SDLs

🌐 Big Pharma + Cloud Lab / Software Integration

Many large pharma companies are accelerating SDL adoption by integrating AI software platforms rather than building full in-house systems from scratch.

Boehringer Ingelheim & OpenProtein.AI

OpenProtein.AI, a small startup founded in 2021, develops AI models for protein design. Its PoET-2 model learns from experimental data and iteratively improves designs. When connected to automated lab systems, it enables a fully closed-loop, end-to-end SDL.

Its cloud platform follows a SaaS subscription model and is currently free for academic users; industry pricing has not been publicly disclosed.

In 2025, Boehringer Ingelheim began collaborating with OpenProtein.AI, and the partnership was expanded in March 2026 to co-develop custom antibody discovery and optimization workflows.

Eli Lilly & NVIDIA

At the J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference in January 2026, Eli Lilly and NVIDIA announced a co-innovation AI lab. The two companies will jointly invest up to $1 billion over five years in infrastructure, talent, and research.

The collaboration aims to build a closed-loop system:

  • NVIDIA contributes supercomputing infrastructure (LillyPod) and its BioNeMo life sciences AI platform.
  • Eli Lilly provides its automated, agentic wet labs.

Experimental data are fed back to AI models in real time, which then generate new predictions and send them back to the lab for testing, forming a continuous 24/7 closed-loop experimentation system.
