In February of this year, Japan’s Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) granted conditional and time-limited marketing approval for two regenerative medicine products derived from induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) during a meeting of its Pharmaceutical Affairs Council.

This decision was made under Japan’s fast-track regulatory framework, the Sakigake Designation System (先駆け審査指定制度), introduced in 2014. Under this system, designated drugs can receive conditional market approval after the completion of Phase I and II trials, followed by a 7-year post-marketing observation period to verify efficacy and safety. This is somewhat analogous to a post-marketing Phase IV study in the United States.

As noted in recent news reports, this accelerated approach has sparked international discussion. However, given that iPSC technology originated at Kyoto University under Nobel laureate Shinya Yamanaka (山中伸彌), it remains one of Japan’s most established and supported biomedical domains.

Amchepry

  • Indication: Parkinson’s disease
  • Developer: Sumitomo Pharma (with RACTHERA)
  • Manufacturing: S-RACMO (joint venture between Sumitomo Chemical and Sumitomo Pharma, specializing in regenerative medicine process development and production.
  • Technology & Development: Kyoto University / CiRA (Center for iPS Cell Research and Application)

Mechanism:
Donor blood cells are reprogrammed into iPSCs (international name: raguneprocel) and then differentiated into dopaminergic neural progenitor cells (DANPCs), which are transplanted into the patient’s brain.

Clinical data:
A Phase I/II study conducted at Kyoto University involved 7 patients aged 50–69. Each received 5–10 million cells implanted bilaterally into the brain.

Two-year longitudinal data demonstrated:

  • Cells successfully engrafted and survived
  • No serious adverse events observed
  • 4 patients showed notable symptomatic improvement, including reduced tremors

Press release: Announcement on the Approval for Manufacturing and Marketing Authorization of the Allogeneic iPS Cell-Derived Dopaminergic Neural Progenitor Cell Product “AMCHEPRY” in Japan

ReHeart

  • Indication: Ischemic cardiomyopathy
  • Developer: Cuorips (クオリプス)

Mechanism:
iPSCs are differentiated into cardiomyocytes to form coin-sized cell sheets (~100 million cells each). These myocardial cell patches are applied to the surface of the patient’s heart via a minimally invasive procedure.

  • The patch naturally adheres to the heart and reaches a stable state within 10–15 minutes.
  • Angiogenesis occurs within approximately 6 hours, as new blood vessels start to grow from the patient’s heart and extend into the patches.
  • The cells also release healing substances that support tissue repair and cardiac recovery.

Clinical data: The Phase I study involving 8 patients demonstrated a favourable safety profile, with some patients showing improved physical capacity.

Press release: World’s First iPSC-Derived Cardiomyocyte Therapy for Heart Failure Receives Conditional Approval in Japan

Cuorips was founded in 2017. The following year, Daiichi Sankyo (第一三共株式会社), KYOTO-iCAP, Terumo Corp, and Mitsubishi Corporation acquired equity stakes.

This equity structure reflects a highly complementary strategic consortium:

  • Daiichi Sankyo: Commercialization and market access
  • KYOTO-iCAP: Technology transfer and early-stage development
  • Terumo: Manufacturing scale-up and process engineering
  • Mitsubishi Corporation: Strategic capital and financial backing

KYOTO-iCAP, a venture capital firm established and operated in late 2014 by Kyoto University, functions similarly to Canada's adMare BioInnovations. Its role involves matching tech developers with investors and assisting in company formation and commercialization.

Japan’s Sakigake Designation System

The Sakigake Designation System was established under the 2014 "Japan Revitalization Strategy (日本再興戦略)" to promote the practical application of innovative drugs, medical devices, and regenerative medical products. To qualify, products must possess "breakthrough" potential, target serious diseases, and demonstrate high efficacy. The objective is to resolve development and drug lags, incentivizing global enterprises to select Japan as the primary launch market for First-in-Class (FIC) products.

Jointly piloted by the MHLW and the PMDA (Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency) in 2015, the system was formally incorporated into regulations following the 2019 revision of the PMD Act (薬機法). Its goal is to position Japan as a first-launch market for innovative therapies.

Japan’s Regulatory System

To qualify, a therapy must meet 4 criteria:

  • Innovativeness: A novel mechanism of action or the first-in-indication application of an existing mechanism.
  • Severity of the target disease: Diseases that pose a significant threat to life, that lack curative treatments, or that severely impair the patient's ability to maintain a normal life.
  • High expected efficacy: Anticipated to offer substantial improvements in efficacy or safety compared to existing treatments, or addressing conditions where no approved drug currently exists.
  • Early development and filing in Japan: The initial submission must be in Japan or synchronized globally. It is highly preferred that First-In-Human (FIH) and Proof-Of-Concept (POC) trials are conducted in Japan.

Benefits & Notes include:

  • Priority Consultation: Prioritized face-to-face guidance and consultation with the PMDA.
  • Pre-evaluation: To limit the formal review period to under 6 months, sponsors must submit all review items during Sakigake consultations prior to the formal application submission.
  • Priority Review: Once designated as a Sakigake drug, it is prioritized for review, shortening the timeline to approximately 6 months compared to the conventional regulatory track (12 months).
  • Concierge: The PMDA assigns a dedicated manager to coordinate communication between the MHLW and the PMDA, providing guidance on development timeline management and facilitating alignment among review departments.

Sumitomo Pharma’s Amchepry received Sakigake Designation in February 2017. It was later designated as an orphan regenerative medicine product in December 2025, enabling it to obtain manufacturing and marketing approval in March 2026 under the conditional approval pathway for a 7-year term.

While official documentation does not explicitly state that ReHeart received a Sakigake designation, it likely followed a similar accelerated regenerative pathway.

iPSC Development Outside Japan

While Japan leads in regulatory acceleration, the U.S. is progressing more conservatively. Several notable iPSC therapies have recently entered clinical trials in the US:

CT1-DAP001 (DSP-1083)

  • Indication: Parkinson's disease
  • Developer: UCSD / Sumitomo Pharma
  • Mechanism: Similar to Amchepry, donor blood cells are converted to iPSCs, differentiated into dopaminergic neural progenitor cells, and transplanted into the brain.
  • Clinical Status: Currently undergoing Phase I/II trials in the US involving 7 Parkinson's patients aged 40 to 75. (NCT06482268)

DSP-3077

  • Indication: Retinitis pigmentosa
  • Developer: Sumitomo Pharma
  • Mechanism: Donor somatic cells are reprogrammed into iPSCs and differentiated into 3D retinal organoids using the SFEBq method. These are processed into retinal sheets (DSP-3077) rich in photoreceptor precursors.
  • Clinical Status: Currently in Phase 1/2 clinical trials. (NCT06891885)

Sasineprocel (ANPD001)

  • Indication: Parkinson's disease
  • Developer: Aspen Neuroscience
  • Mechanism: Autologous skin cells are reprogrammed into iPSCs and differentiated into dopaminergic neural progenitor cells (DANPCs) for surgical implantation.
  • Clinical Status: Phase 1/2a clinical trials are ongoing with nine patients aged 50 to 70. Primary endpoints assess safety at 1 and 5 years post-transplantation. (NCT06344026)

OpCT-001

  • Indication: Retinal degeneration
  • Developer: BlueRock (Bayer) / FUJIFILM Cellular Dynamics / Opsis Therapeutics
  • Mechanism: Donor somatic cells are converted to iPSCs, differentiated into photoreceptor cells, and implanted into the eye.
  • Clinical Status: Currently in Phase I clinical trials. (NCT06789445)

Fertilo

  • Indication: Infertility / IVF Optimization
  • Developer: Gameto
  • Mechanism: In vitro maturation (IVM). iPSCs are differentiated into ovarian support cells (OSCs) to recreate the follicular environment, maturing naturally retrieved immature oocytes outside the body.
  • Clinical Status: Phase III trials initiated in early 2025 following the first successful birth utilizing Fertilo in late 2024. (NCT06858111)

Investigational IVG Pipeline

  • Indication: Infertility / Ovarian Insufficiency
  • Developer: Ovelle Bio
  • Mechanism: In vitro oogenesis (IVG). Reprogramming human iPSCs to induce meiosis and generate meiotic oogonia-like cells. These are incorporated into ovarian organoids to create synthetic ovaries capable of producing entirely new oocytes from somatic cells.
  • Clinical Status: Preclinical / Early technological development.

Trends & Bottlenecks in iPSC Therapies in the past 20 years

Recent data from ClinicalTrials.gov indicates a surge in iPSC therapies entering clinical trials in 2025, predominantly targeting neurological disorders. While earlier studies were largely observational, the volume of interventional trials has increased significantly since 2021.

Market research projects the cell therapy market to reach approximately $2 billion in 2025, with an estimated growth to $5 billion by 2035. However, several operational and technical bottlenecks remain:

  • High Costs: iPSC therapies are either autologous or allogeneic. Autologous (personalized) therapies mitigate immune rejection but carry costs exceeding $100,000 per patient and face severe scalability limitations. Allogeneic therapies are scalable but present immune rejection risks.
  • Product Purity: Unlike small molecules or monoclonal antibodies, maintaining cellular homogeneity during differentiation and isolating successfully differentiated cells from heterogeneous populations remains technically complex.
  • Tumorigenic Risk: The reprogramming and differentiation processes carry the risk of genetic mutations, potentially leading to tumorigenicity.
  • Regulatory Hurdles: US FDA pathways typically require Phase III clinical data prior to approval, offering higher patient safety assurances but delaying market access compared to Japan's accelerated Phase 1/2 conditional approval framework.

Can iPSC Therapies Be Profitable?

Existing models provide precedents for cell therapy commercialization. Despite market saturation, these therapies generate substantial revenue:

  • Kymriah (Novartis): Generated $443 million in 2024, maintaining profitability despite revenue declines since 2021.
  • Yescarta (Gilead/Kite): Remains a dominant market leader, generating $1.5 billion in 2025.
  • Carvykti (J&J/Legend): Surpassed Yescarta with $1.9 billion in 2025 revenue.

Factors driving profitability in CAR-T include:

  • Scalability and cost reduction via automated processing
  • Premium pricing models
  • Positioning as a first-line therapy

iPSC therapies could theoretically replicate this commercial trajectory, albeit with higher technical and regulatory risks.

Strategic Outlook

Japan’s regulatory paradigm structurally prioritizes accelerated access over definitive early certainty in regenerative medicine approval, leveraging its leadership in iPSC technology.

Whether this approach proves visionary or premature will depend on the real-world data collected over the next 7 years.
